Session 22 - The Demon Core

Session Summary

In-Game Date: Fall, 1949
Dimension: 45

Key Events

  • Arrival in Dimension 45:
    The heroes emerge disoriented from the flash into an elegant office flanked by portraits of Boss Tom Pendergast and Al Capone. The men depicted sit in front of their portraits in large ornamental chairs. With them is Dr. Ellie Rothschild, a striking, red-haired physicist in a lab coat and horn-rimmed glasses. She welcomes them, explaining that she pulled the team into her universe to prevent a catastrophe she’s foreseen through her research.

  • Ellie Rothschild and the Demon Core:
    Ellie reveals she worked on the Manhattan Project, where an accident wiped out everyone else in her lab and at the desert site. She alone survived — along with the plutonium sphere known historically as the Demon Core. The object hums faintly with atomic energy, an artifact of a universe where the atomic bomb has not yet been developed.

    • Ellie suspects she may be related to Madman Markham.
    • She believes each timeline she’s observed ends disastrously unless this group intervenes.

  • A Fractured 1949:
    The date is 1949. The U.S. faces Japanese and German incursions, still locked in a war that never properly ended.

    • The heroes’ mission becomes entangled with Ellie’s effort to locate Tokyo Rose, whose transmissions seem to manipulate fate itself.
    • Despite triangulation attempts, her location remains elusive.

  • Captain France Tests the Limits:
    While investigating Ellie’s setup, Captain France tests his power by punching a bulkhead, tearing it apart and exposing the room to radiation. Ellie manages to power everything down before harm spreads, but the event proves the instability of this dimension’s physics — and the latent danger of the Demon Core.

  • New Faces and Familiar Echoes:
    The team is given a driver, a wiry young man named Wheels, who ferries them around this noir-era Kansas City. Each hero finds echoes of their own lives in this alternate history:

    • YMCA (Blurry’s analogue): A letter from “home” awaits — the tractor’s broken, the tone nostalgic and personal.

    • Manifester: Lives above a seamstress and costume shop. The phonograph skips endlessly on “Take the A Train.” Notes in his own handwriting read:

      “Check out Cara Cadaver’s act — two days from now.” and “Don’t touch my stuff.”

    • Captain France: His home contains a secret radio room, tuned to Free French resistance signals. A U.S. officer named Tom (likely Pendergast’s agent) monitors from outside. He also finds a note. It reads

      “Don’t trust Pendergast. He’s not all he appears.”

    • Automatic Man: Detects that his power source feels different — arcane rather than technological. A pentagram glows faintly on his chestplate.

  • Technological Landscape:
    Mechanical beings exist here but are primitive compared to Automatic Man’s design — stiff, unsophisticated, running on tubes and clockwork. His mere presence is decades ahead of this dimension’s science.

  • Return to the Majestic:
    The team visits the Majestic Theater, finding a familiar yet reimagined setting.

    • A woman named Lovely Louisa, assistant to Manifester’s local counterpart, greets them and flirts openly with Captain France.
    • The team gathers intelligence over drinks until word spreads of gunfire atop Bartle Hall. They rush to investigate.

  • The Rooftop Mystery:
    On the rooftop, they find nothing but spent high-caliber casings and bullet holes—signs of a brief but fierce fight. No bodies. No shooters. The trail leads nowhere… yet.

  • Closing Glimpse:
    As they descend, the Fountain of Buck and Honor gleams in the dusk — a monument to an even older time. Word reaches them that Templar is alive in Europe, fighting on the front lines of a world that never found peace.

Heroes

  • Manifester — Gains self-authored note linking him to Cara Cadaver’s act; reestablishes base above the seamstress shop.
  • Captain France — Tests new powers; nearly breaches containment; shows connection to Free French efforts via hidden radio.
  • Automatic Man — Feels transformed energy signature; discovers pentagram sigil on chest.
  • Templar — Alive in Europe; word of his presence spreads among Allied forces.

Villains and Threats

  • Pendergast — Political boss and potential meta-human manipulator; presence evokes suspicion.
  • Tokyo Rose — Signal source of psychic broadcasts affecting causality.
  • The Demon Core — Still active; possible dimensional anchor or temporal hazard.
  • German and Japanese Forces — Ongoing warfront pressure, cause of chaos and instability.

Other Characters

  • Dr. Ellie Rothschild — Red-haired scientist; Manhattan Project survivor; possible ancestor to Madman Markham; custodian of the Demon Core.
  • Boss Tom Pendergast — Power broker; uncertain motives.
  • Al Capone — Shares control of “The Clubhouse”; pragmatic and dangerous.
  • Lovely Louisa — Manifester’s assistant; playful, observant, possibly more than she seems.
  • Wheels — Quick-talking driver.

Notes

  • Dimension 45 diverges post–WWII, 1949 U.S. still at war.
  • Ellie’s “timelines always end badly unless you intervene” implies some multiversal resonance tied to the team.
  • The Demon Core’s continued presence may be the anchor that pulled them here.
  • The notes left for the heroes suggest alternate selves aware of the crossover before it happened.
  • Templar’s survival sets up future reconnection or rescue arc.
